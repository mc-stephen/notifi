---
title: How the webhook relay works
description: One connect posts your rendered payload anywhere you point it.
---

## The short version

The **Webhook relay** card (under the "Other" channel) delivers your
notification by calling a URL of YOUR choosing, shaped however that receiver
wants: **we push to you** — Notifi POSTs (or GETs, or whatever the receiver
expects) and your system receives. It is the counterpart of the gateway card
next to it, which is the reverse direction: your system listens to us by
polling a hosted route. The relay is the escape hatch for anything Notifi
does not have a built-in card for yet — your own webhook target, a
self-hosted tool, an internal service.

## What you set up on the card

Connecting the relay collects four things, and they are all saved into the
card's recipe:

1. **Target URL** — where the request goes.
2. **Method** — picked from a dropdown (POST, GET, PUT, PATCH, DELETE — no
   mistypings; POST is the usual).
3. **Headers** — optional request headers, e.g. an `Authorization` or a
   signature line your receiver wants (they can be given as an object, a JSON
   string, or left empty).
4. **Payload template** — the body, written once and rendered per send with
   Handlebars-style placeholders. The form starts you from
   `{"title": "{{title}}", "message": "{{body}}"}` — the helper line under
   the field names the four placeholders:

   - `{{title}}` — the notification title
   - `{{body}}` — the notification text
   - `{{project}}` — the sending project
   - `{{timestamp}}` — when the relay fires

   Example template:
   `{"title": "{{title}}", "message": "{{body}}", "source": "{{project}}"}`

There are no vendor credentials — the URL is the provider. That is why only
one "Other → Relay" card exists: one connect, many receivers.

## What a send looks like

On each send, Notifi renders your template with the notification's content
and calls your target URL with your headers and method. One send, one request
— no retry storm.
