---
title: How the Notifi gateway works
description: Notifi hosts the listening path — your app (any OS, any script) polls its secret route.
---

## The short version

The Notifi gateway is a hosted pull-route: Notifi queues your notifications,
and your app collects them by polling one URL. No listener for you to run —
your app is a client, not a server. It is the counterpart of our webhook
relay (where we push to you): with the gateway, your system listens to ours.

It lives on the **Other** section of the providers page, and it is not
platform-specific — a Linux daemon, a Windows service, a macOS script, a cron
job that can make an HTTPS call can all poll the same shape of route.

## What you set up on the card

Nothing. Connecting generates the whole route for you:

- **Route topic** — generated by Notifi (you'll see it as the path piece of
  your subscribe URL; there's nothing to pick or type).
- **Route secret** — generated too (prefilled readonly, with a regenerate
  button if you want a fresh one).
- **Delivery mode** — the one real choice, how you plan to poll:
   - **Web polling (recommended)** — your app's request holds the connection a
     little bit longer (`?wait=20`) and the message lands the instant it is
     queued. Lowest latency, tiny polling traffic.
   - **HTTPS check** — no holding. Your app checks whenever it wants (a cron
     job, a loop on its own cadence) and gets whatever is queued right away.

The saved mode is just a note; the mode is really your app's per-request
choice, so you can switch behavior any time without reconnecting.

## The URL your app polls

After connecting, the form shows the subscribe line, e.g.:

```
https://api.notifi.dev/gateway/gw-xk4p2m9tq7v3?key=<generated secret>
```

- Poll it with an ordinary HTTPS GET.
- To long-poll, append `&wait=20` (the server holds up to 20 seconds and
  answers as soon as something queues).
- The reply is JSON: `{"messages": [ { "id", "title", "body", "received_at" } ]}`.
  Empty queue → an empty list.

## Reads are one-shot (poll-and-delete)

A fetched message is ALSO removed at the same moment — a message leaves its
route at first read. That keeps the story simple: no retries, no duplicates
accumulating, no caching to babysit. If your app crashes mid-delivery, that
message is gone; the next send starts fresh. If you need stored history,
capture it on your side when you receive it.

## How sends find your route

Both route halves (topic and secret) live in the saved card recipe. When a
flow or send targets the gateway, Notifi queues the message onto exactly that
topic+secret pair — the queue needs no registry of its own, and a wrong key
just sees an empty queue.
